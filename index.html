Why companies leave their CRM

By the time a company calls us, they have usually done the math on the bill, whether it comes from Salesforce, Zoho, or another vendor. The same three frustrations come up in almost every conversation.

The cost grows with you

Per-seat pricing means every new hire raises the bill, whether they use one feature or fifty. A system you own costs what it costs to build and run. Headcount stops being a line item.

The process bends to the tool

A packaged CRM has its own idea of how a sale should run, so your team works around it, and even adding a field means waiting on an admin or paying a consultant. Software built around your process shouldn't need an interpreter.

Your data lives on their platform

Your records and your workflow live inside someone else's product, so every renewal is a negotiation you can't walk away from. Ownership hands the leverage back.

The big CRMs earn their price at companies that use most of what they do. If your team lives in a handful of screens and exports to spreadsheets for the rest, you are paying for software you don't use, and a system built around your actual process will cost less to run.

Renting vs. owning

The argument, side by side.

Topic Renting a CRM A system you own
What it costs Per seat, per month, for as long as you use it. The bill grows with headcount. A build cost, then hosting and support. New hires cost nothing.
Making a change Wait for an admin, or pay a consultant to add a field. Ask the developers who built it. Most changes are small jobs.
Your data Stored on their servers, in their format. In a database you control, exported whenever you like.
At renewal They name the price. Walking away means an unplanned migration. There is no renewal.

Where we fit, and where we don't

Custom software is a good decision for some companies and a bad one for others. Here is how to tell which you are.

A good fit

  • Your process already works. The tools around it are the problem.
  • The CRM bill, or the spreadsheet workaround, has a monthly cost you can name.
  • Someone on your team can own the project and give it an hour or two a week.
  • You expect to run this system for five years or more.

Not a good fit

  • You need something live in a month. Good custom work takes longer than that.
  • Your team uses the deep end of Salesforce. If you would miss CPQ or the AppExchange, stay.
  • You are collecting bids and plan to take the lowest one. That is rarely us.
  • Nobody on your side has time to look at working software while it is being built.

Talk it through before you decide anything.
