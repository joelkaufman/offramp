A planned, low-drama exit from Salesforce

Two worries stall every Salesforce exit: that the team will lose a week of work, and that records will go missing in the move. Our process was built around both. Your team keeps working in Salesforce until the new system is proven, and nothing cuts over until the data checks out against the source.

What keeps the switch safe

No big-bang switch

Your new system is built while Salesforce keeps running. Nothing changes for your team until the replacement has been tested against real work, side by side.

Data moved, then proved

Every object, field, and attachment is exported, mapped, and migrated in rehearsals before the real move. Record counts and spot checks are reconciled until they match, and you keep a complete archive of the original data.

A team that's ready

Your people train on the new system with your actual data before cutover day, and there's a rollback plan in writing. The switch happens when you say it does.

Built by people who know Salesforce

The engineers who build your replacement know Salesforce from the inside. They've built on its object model and untangled heavily customized orgs, so nothing in your setup needs explaining before work starts.

That experience matters most during the migration. Someone who already knows how Salesforce stores your records reads an export correctly the first time and recognizes the quirks before they turn into missing data. The new system ends up shaped around what your org really does, because the people designing it can read the org themselves.

The process, step by step

  1. Audit

    We map what your Salesforce org actually does: the objects, fields, reports, automations, and integrations in real use, and the ones nobody has touched in years. You get a written plan covering what the new system needs, what the migration involves, and how it lines up with your renewal date.

  2. Build alongside Salesforce

    We build the replacement while your team keeps working exactly as before. You review working software as it takes shape, so the system fits your process before anyone depends on it.

  3. Migrate the data

    Accounts, contacts, pipeline, history, attachments: everything is exported, mapped to the new system, and moved in trial runs. Each rehearsal is reconciled against the source, counts and spot checks alike, until the migration is boring. Boring is the goal.

  4. Cut over

    On a date you choose, with your team trained and a rollback plan on paper, work moves to the new system. A final sync carries over anything that changed since the last rehearsal.

  5. Support

    We stay on after the switch to fix what the first weeks of real use surface, and later to extend the system as your process changes. You own the code either way.

What you leave with

Reconciliation reports

A rollback plan

Agreed on paper before cutover day, so a bad surprise has a known answer.

Questions we hear most

What happens to our Salesforce data?

All of it is exported before anything else happens, and you keep a complete archive regardless of what moves into the new system. The working data is mapped and migrated in rehearsals, and each rehearsal is reconciled against the source until the numbers match.

How long does a migration take?

It depends on how much your org actually does: a lean sales pipeline is a different project from a decade of customization and integrations. That's what the audit is for. It ends with a written plan with dates, so the honest answer arrives early and in writing rather than as a surprise later.

Our renewal date is coming up. When should we start?

Before it, with as much runway as you can give the project. The audit sizes the work against your renewal so you know whether to plan the cutover ahead of it or renew once more, possibly with fewer seats, while the replacement is built.

What about our integrations and reports?

The audit inventories both. Integrations are rebuilt against the new system or connected directly, and the reports people actually use are recreated, usually simpler, because the system now matches the process.

What if something is wrong after cutover?

There's a rollback plan agreed before the switch, your Salesforce org remains available for as long as your contract runs, and we stay on through a support period to fix whatever the first weeks of real use turn up.

The price of leaving just dropped

For years the strongest case for staying was the cost of the replacement. A custom build meant a team of engineers working for months, and the quote could rival several years of Salesforce seats. For plenty of companies the honest advice was to sit tight.

AI-assisted development changed that advice. Our engineers now build with AI handling most of the routine code, and recent projects have come in as low as a tenth of what the same system would have cost a few years ago. Salesforce's pricing has not moved in that direction. With the upfront investment that small, the switch becomes a short payback calculation, and every renewal you skip after it is money that stays with you.
